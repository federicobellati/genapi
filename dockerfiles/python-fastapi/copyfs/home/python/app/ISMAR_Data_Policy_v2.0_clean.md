Introduction

The  Institute  of  Marine  Sciences  (ISMAR)  of  the  National  Research  Council  of  Italy  (CNR)  conducts
fundamental and applied research in the fields of physical, chemical and biological oceanography, ecology,
and marine geology. The institute contributes to understanding ocean processes and climate variability, as
well as to the development of systems and services for observing, forecasting, protecting, and sustainably
managing the marine environment and its resources. CNR-ISMAR produces various types of data (e.g. in the
ecological,  oceanographic,  geophysical  and  geological  domains)  in  the  form  of,  among  others,  spatial
datasets and long-term time series, that are the result of ocean observation and multidisciplinary research.

CNR-ISMAR adopts a data access policy that complies with the open-access and FAIR principles (Findable,
Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) established in national and EU legislation, in line with the institution’s
mission, structure and organisation. In general, the policy is guided by four key elements, with the objective
of making data “as open as possible”:

1.  data is produced with public funds and therefore public use and reuse should be encouraged;
2.  open data allows for faster advancement of knowledge with a positive effect on research results;
3.  sharing promotes the visibility and scientific output of the individual researchers who generated the

data;

4.  sharing also enables the reuse of data by third parties with objectives that may differ from those of
the  data  producers,  thereby  enhancing  the  overall  value  and  impact.  In  addition,  such  reuse  can
support the development of new research based on this data, for example by making bathymetric
data available for biological, oceanographic, or geological studies.

This document defines the policy for accessing data and information made available by CNR-ISMAR, with the
aim of facilitating its dissemination, access and use within the institute and beyond. The document sets out
guidelines for sharing data and information in order to strike a balance between the rights of those who
produce it and the need for free, open, and unrestricted sharing and exchange.
The main objectives of the CNR-ISMAR Data Policy are:

1.  to  promote  the  quality,  integrity  and  reliability  of  scientific  data  produced  within  CNR-ISMAR

projects/campaigns;

2.  to ensure the security and confidentiality of sensitive data;
3.  to encourage the sharing and accessibility of data among researchers and the scientific community;
4.  to ensure compliance with data protection regulations and best practices.

This document (and the definitions therein) is consistent with European Directive 2003/4/EC on public access
to  environmental  information  [1],  European  Directive  2019/1024  on  open  data  [2]  and  its  implementing

Definitions

CNR-ISMAR adopts the following definitions in order to assign an unambiguous meaning to the terms used.

Authorship
Authorship of the data is reserved for individuals who contributed to one or more of the following phases:
experimental design or planning of the data collection campaign, data collection in the field or laboratory,
data processing and quality control (QC/QA), data analysis and interpretation, or creation of derived datasets
or scientific products. It implies accountability for the integrity of the work.

Data
In this document the term “data” refers to digital data classified according to their level of processing as
follows:

1.  Raw data (also referred to as source or atomic data): information that has not been processed to be
presented  in  any  interpretable  or  presentable  form.  Raw  Data  are  the  unprocessed,  original
measurements  or  observations  collected from  experiments,  surveys,  or simulations,  in their  most
basic form. They have not undergone cleaning, calibration, or interpretation. Examples include raw
environmental parameters with ancillary data, sequencing data, seismic shot gathers, data resulting
from routine numerical simulations, etc…;

2.  Processed data:data derived from Raw Data through cleaning, calibration, transformation, or quality
control  procedures  that  make  the  data  interpretable  and  suitable  for  reuse.  Examples  include
calibrated and quality-controlled environmental data, non-standard chemical tracer data (e.g. rare
earth elements, isotopic ratios, organic pollutants), data produced from in situ or in-lab experiments,
seismic reflection profiles, data derived from numerical experiments, numerical models, bathymetry
and terrain surveys in digital format, statistical processing, meta-analysis, molecular sequence data;
3.  Products:  intellectual outputs derived from analysis, interpretation, or modeling of Raw or Processed
Data, such as theoretical or physical models, data related to biological communities and populations,
(both  automatically  and  manually),  synthetic  graphical
interpreted  and  processed  data
representations, thematic maps, 3D geological models, processed core analysis data, codes, maps
and atlases in digital and non-digital format, etc…

Data Management Plan (DMP)
The DMP outlines how data will be collected, processed, shared, and preserved throughout the research
lifecycle.

Data Owner
Data owners are entities that either generate data without external constraints or finance data generation
under specific contractual conditions. They hold ownership of the data, ensure their quality, and have the
authority to define access and preservation policies.

Data Producer
Data producers are individuals or groups who conduct observations and/or experiments and generate data
and metadata. They organize this data, either directly or after processing, in accordance with policies defined
by the data owner, into records that can be shared and used by third parties.

Data quality
The extent to which data are accurate, complete, consistent reliable, up to date, and fit for purpose.

Data Steward
A Data Steward is a professional specialized in the responsible and ethical planning and management of data
(data stewardship) and plays a crucial role in promoting Open Science. Acting as an intermediary, the Data
Steward provides technical, disciplinary, and legal support for data management. This role involves close
collaboration with data producers to ensure that research data are managed responsibly and in compliance
with  privacy  regulations  and  intellectual  property  rights.  The  Data  Steward  may  assist  data  producers  in
identifying  best  practices  for  data  management,  ensuring  accessibility  and  sharing,  and  guaranteeing
compliance with data policies.

General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
A comprehensive European Union law, effective May 25, 2018, that governs how personal data is collected,
used, and protected. It gives individuals greater control over their data, applies to any organization handling
EU residents' data, and carries high fines for breaches.

Intellectual Property
Intellectual property refers to the body of legal rights aimed at protecting the outcomes of human creativity
and  intellectual  endeavour.  Intellectual  property rights  are  distinct from authorship attribution  and  from
licensing terms governing data reuse.

Legacy Data
Legacy data refers to data “inherited” from the past, collected before the introduction of formal policies for
data management, preservation, and sharing (not originally managed according to FAIR principles or a formal
Data Management Plan). In this document, legacy data refers to data collected before the introduction of
the first version of the CNR-ISMAR data policy (02/10/2023).
Legacy data includes also scientific information stored in obsolete formats or on outdated media, often
lacking the standardized metadata required for modern validation or integration into current open-science
frameworks.

License
A license is a legal agreement between the data producer/owner and the user, defining the conditions under
which data may be used (e.g. Creative Commons, see Annex I). It represents a necessary legal instrument to
protect against improper use by unauthorized parties or misuse that infringes intellectual property rights.

Metadata
Metadata is a descriptive information that accompanies data, enhances, and completes their understanding
by providing details on their meaning, context, provenance, and quality. Metadata is essential to enable data
discovery, interpretation, and effective use and re-use by other users.

Moral rights
Moral rights include the right of attribution, i.e. the right of the author to be identified and recognised as the
creator of the work.

Moratorium or Embargo Period
A period of time granted to data producers to process their data before making them public and available
for  sharing  with  the  community.  The  application  and  duration  of  the  moratorium  must  comply  with
institutional rules and applicable project agreements.

Open Access
In general, Open Access refers to the practice of providing access to data and metadata without additional
costs for the end user, apart from marginal costs related to reproduction and dissemination, and allowing
its reuse.

Persistent Identifier (PID)
A  Persistent  Identifier  is  an  alphanumeric  code  whose  structure  is  defined  within  object  management
systems (e.g. for data, books, people, physical samples), some of which follow internationally shared open

<!-- Pagina originale: 7 -->

standards (e.g. DOI, ORCID, IGSN). The term “persistent” refers to the ability to ensure that the association
between the identifier and the web location of the associated object is maintained over time.

Project Participant (PP): all participants included in the research project in which the data was collected. A
project participant can request access to data during the moratorium period.

Project:  any  funded  and  non-funded  research  project  including  regional,  national  and  international
programs, curiosity-driven proposals.

User
Users  include  both  natural  persons  and  legal  entities,  such  as  research  institutes,  public  or  private
organisations, associations, and companies, that request access to data for different purposes.

CNR-ISMAR data policy principles

In principle, and subject to the exceptions specifically indicated in the User Licences, CNR-ISMAR makes data
available timely, freely and without restrictions: “timely” means sufficiently rapidly to be of value for a given
application,  “freely”  means  without  charge  for  the  data  itself;  “without  restrictions”  means  without
discrimination against, for example, individuals, research groups, or nationalities, or on the basis of purposes
that may also be commercial, and in any case in compliance with current legislation on the reuse of public
sector information (Directive 2019/1024 of the European Parliament) [2]. However, these principles may not
apply  where  access  or  reuse  must  be  restricted  under  applicable  law  including  EU  sanctions  and  public
security measures adopted in response to armed conflict or international emergencies.

CNR-ISMAR makes data available to users, using sharing tools to be defined according to the type of data
and favouring the  use  of  open  formats and  recognised  standards. CNR-ISMAR  encourages the  sharing of
scientific data through trusted repositories, and/or through institutional archives or platforms, in order to
promote scientific collaboration and data accessibility. The specific methods and tools for sharing data is the
subject of the CNR-ISMAR Data Management Plan (DMP) [17].

The sharing conditions vary depending on whether metadata or data is involved:

1) metadata is always timely and freely accessible without any restrictions and is associated with a Creative
Commons CC-BY 4.0 license;

<!-- Pagina originale: 8 -->

2) data,  unless otherwise specified in applicable agreements or institutional provisions, are made available
under a CC-BY 4.0 license.   The Data Producer, in coordination with the Institute and in compliance with
applicable  agreements,  must  ensure  adherence  to  the  principles  of  the  CNR-ISMAR  Data  Policy  and
consistency with national and international projects and the guidelines of European Research Infrastructures
(RIs).  Access to data may be subject to restrictions relating to intellectual property, privacy, confidentiality,
or other ethical considerations (e.g. protection of rare or endangered species, national security), contractual
obligations with private entities, or agreements with  partner institutions. The data shall not be used, directly
or indirectly, for any military, defense, intelligence, or warfare-related purpose.
All personal data shall be handled in compliance with the GDPR [18], with appropriate safeguards to ensure
privacy, security, limited retention, and the protection of data subjects’ rights.

Any redistribution or sharing of the Data, in original or modified form, must include these use restrictions as
legally enforceable terms, and the recipient must be informed that use of the Data is subject to the same
prohibitions.

The applicable license or any other access and reuse conditions shall be formally encoded in the metadata.
The data steward may provide assistance  in determining the most suitable conditions for access and reuse,
in alignment with institutional regulations.

This Data Policy applies as a general framework to all data generated within CNR-ISMAR activities. In cases
where data are generated within externally funded projects, commercial contracts, or formal collaborations
with other institutions, data ownership, access conditions, intellectual property rights, and reuse terms may
be  governed  by  specific  contractual  agreements.  Where  such  agreements  exist  and  have  been  formally
approved by the Institute, their provisions shall prevail over this Policy, within the limits of applicable laws
and institutional regulations.

Moratorium periods

A defined time interval during which access to data generated within CNR-ISMAR activities is restricted to
the Data Producer and/or authorized institutional personnel before public release.  A moratorium period is
established  starting  from  the  date  the  data  is  generated,  during  which  it  remains  available  only  to  the
project participants. For data produced by research projects or contracts and governed by the relevant Data
Policy, the moratorium period starts from the end of the project/contract.

In general, the following maximum moratorium periods apply to data:

<!-- Pagina originale: 9 -->

•  12-month moratorium (or the minimum time necessary to transfer the data/products in a format

suitable for use by third parties) for Raw data.

•  24-month moratorium for Processed data.
•  36-month moratorium for Data products.

For seismic data, the following moratorium periods are applied:

•  24-month moratorium for Raw data.
•  48-month moratorium for Processed data and products.

Although it is not possible to establish a moratorium period for legacy data (see definition above) due to
objective difficulties in cataloguing, retrieval and reproduction, all data producers are required to guarantee
the  safeguard  of  such  data  by  producing  metadata  within  12  months  once  the  institutional  metadata
procedure  is  available.  Data  producers  are  required  to  share  legacy  data  as  soon  as  possible,  once  the
institutional repository will be fully implemented.

Data ownership and intellectual property

Data generated during research, unless agreed otherwise in writing (e.g., by contract), is the property of the
Institute of Marine Sciences of the National Research Council of Italy.

In  the  case  of collaborative  research  -  supported  by public  funds -  ownership of the  research  results no
longer lies with the researcher (producer) but with the Institution. In collaborative research, it is assumed
that the  result  is obtained  in  cooperation  with  the partner(s),  and  therefore intellectual  property  can be
shared among the partners according to their contribution.

In  the  case  of  research  within  the  framework  of  research  projects  or  RIs  –  i.e.  supported  by  regional,
national and international research programmes – ownership of the data belongs to the Institution, while
the data policy is that of the project. Intellectual property always remains with the producer.

In the case of commissioned research – i.e. carried out on the basis of a contract – ownership of the research
is  defined  in  the  agreement/contract/convention  stipulated  with  the  organisation.  Intellectual  property
always remains with the producer.

In  unusual  cases  of  independent  research  –  i.e.  research  funded  directly  from  the  researcher's  budget,
without the use of public infrastructure and facilities (hardware and software), and outside of work activities
– the data is owned by the individual researcher, who in this case is both the producer and owner of the

<!-- Pagina originale: 10 -->

data.  The  researcher  may  decide  whether  to  share  the  data  with  the  Institution  (intellectual  property
remains in any case with the producer).

Data producer responsibility

Data Producers are responsible for ensuring the scientific integrity, documentation, and proper description
of  the  data  in  compliance  with  this  Data  Policy  and  applicable  project  agreements.  Data  producers  are
responsible for secure storage of data and regular backups, which are mandatory to ensure data recovery in
case of loss. They are also responsible for providing metadata in a timely manner and for ensuring its quality,
completeness, and consistency. In particular, data producers should guarantee clear and accurate citation
information(following agreed workflows and standards) and respect for moral rights.

Different citation scenarios can be identified:
•  Data within trusted repository:

If the resource is already catalogued and associated with an official citation (e.g. DOI-based), the
same citation should be used without modification.

•  Data described in a scientific publication:

If the resource is presented within a scientific article (e.g. supplementary materials) or in a data
paper, the corresponding publication should be cited.

•  Data produced within projects or RIs:

If data are generated within a research project or a RI, the citation should follow the rules and
formats defined by the respective project or RI.
•  Data that does not fall within the above categories:

If data does not fall within the above categories the data producer should apply a standardized
citation format (e.g. APA style), including authorship, year, title, repository, and persistent identifier
provided by institutional repository.

For CNR-ISMAR data, if authorship cannot be determined due to a lack of information, the institution is to
be cited as follow: National Research Council of Italy - Institute of Marine Sciences (CNR-ISMAR).
This approach ensures traceability, proper attribution, and alignment with international best practices and
FAIR principles.

User responsibility

If data is not shared in trusted repositories and there is no defined workflow for data requests, users can use
the CNR-ISMAR Data Request and Data Policy Acceptance Form (Annex II) to request data directly from the
data  producer.  The  form  should  be  sent  to  the  data  provider  (if  available)  and  to  the  data  owner
(datarequest@ismar.cnr.it).  This  workflow  will  be  replaced  by  a  more  technical  solution  that  will  be
implemented at the CNR-ISMAR level. For datasets available only upon request, the presence of a metadata
catalogue will also facilitate and streamline the request process.

Regardless  of whether the data  is quality  controlled  or not,  CNR-ISMAR accepts  no  responsibility  for  the
accuracy and/or appropriate interpretation of the data. The correct and appropriate interpretation of the
data must follow scientific rules and is always the sole responsibility of the users.

Users are required to acknowledge that ownership of the data remains with the producer/owner of the data
as  per  the  applicable  licence.  The  correct  attribution  of  data  through  citations  and  references  should  be
encouraged, promoting transparency and visibility of the original authors. It is not permitted to publish data
without  the  correct  citation  of  the  owner  and,  where  applicable,  the  relevant  bibliographic  citation
(Persistent Identifier).
Furthermore, users are required to inform CNR-ISMAR of any problems encountered with the data provided,
in order to correct such data and improve its quality.

Concluding remarks

Training and awareness opportunities must be provided to data producers/owners on data management, IT
security, privacy and related legal aspects. Technical, regulatory and legal support in data management will
be provided by the Data Steward, such as choosing the most appropriate licence and compiling metadata
based on the standards most suited to the different types of data.
This  Data  Policy  shall  be  periodically  reviewed  and  updated  as  necessary  to  take  account  of  regulatory,
technological and scientific changes.
